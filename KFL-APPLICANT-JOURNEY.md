## Framework

Same discipline as the First-Year Member Journey: each stage answers what the applicant experiences, what uncertainty should resolve there, and what confidence should be built — not just what page or process exists.

---

## Stage 1 — First Contact

**What the applicant experiences:** Encountering KFL for the first time — referral, direct outreach, or evidence-archive content (per `KFL-TRAFFIC-STRATEGY-90DAY.md`, unchanged). At this point he knows almost nothing concrete.

**What should resolve here:** Almost nothing needs to resolve yet — this stage's only job is producing enough intrigue to warrant a second look. Per Offer Architecture Part I, what should register even at this shallow a glance is the basic shape of the differentiation argument (real lineage + individual attention + a instructor with range), not full detail.

**Confidence built:** None yet, by design. Premature detail at this stage is wasted — Hormozi Synthesis #8 and #9 both argue against overselling before genuine interest exists.

---

## Stage 2 — Deepening Interest

**What the applicant experiences:** Reading further — the site, possibly evidence-archive essays, possibly asking someone who referred him.

**What should resolve here:** Whether this is a martial arts class (it should already be clear it isn't) and whether it's for someone like him specifically — the Filter section's existing "this is/is not for you" mechanism is the right tool for this, and should stay.

**Confidence built:** That the differentiation argument (Offer Architecture Part I) isn't just asserted — that lineage, individual correction, and range are each independently substantiated somewhere on the site, not just claimed in one paragraph.

---

## Stage 3 — Trust

**What the applicant experiences:** The point where he's seriously weighing whether to apply, not just reading. This is where Offer Architecture's five uncertainty-reduction questions have to actually be answered, not just referenced:

1. **What happens after I apply?** → Should be stated plainly before the form, not only in the post-submission confirmation copy (Offer Architecture Part III, Q1) — application reviewed personally by Rafael, a direct call if it advances, decision either way communicated personally.
2. **What does the first year look like?** → Now partially answerable using the First-Year Member Journey's phase structure and, if the Commercial Operating Model is adopted, the concrete Weekly Apprenticeship Session / Individual Guidance Session rhythm. Should be described in outline, not full detail — enough to remove abstractness, not so much that it reads like a syllabus.
3. **What is expected of me, concretely?** → The application's existing commitment-qualifier questions already contain this content; it should also appear as a plain statement before the applicant reaches the form, not only as questions he has to answer.
4. **How is progress measured?** → General, petal-level Plum Blossom description only (per Blueprint Part VIII — the twenty-five-mark detail stays internal).
5. **Why is this different from the alternatives I'm implicitly comparing it to?** → Offer Architecture Part I's differentiation argument, restated in site voice.

**Confidence built:** That applying is a serious, well-defined step with a known shape — not a leap into an undefined process.

---

## Stage 4 — Deciding to Apply

**What the applicant experiences:** The actual decision point — reading the Filter section one more time, deciding whether he's really the kind of man this is for.

**What should resolve here:** Any remaining self-doubt about fit should be *encouraged* to surface here, not suppressed — per V1-BASELINE's original design principle, the Filter section exists specifically to let the wrong man screen himself out before the form, not after Rafael's time is spent on an interview.

**Confidence built:** That applying doesn't obligate him to anything yet — it's a request to be considered, not a purchase.

---

## Stage 5 — The Application

**What the applicant experiences:** Filling out the actual form.

**What needs to change here, concretely (flagging real gaps found):**
- The existing form's development-goals options and commitment-qualifier language were last meaningfully reviewed during the brotherhood-era copy (per Current State Assessment); Client Acquisition Architecture already flagged the tracker's "understands brotherhood concept" field as stale — the *application form itself* should be checked for the same staleness, not just the tracker that scores it.
- Per the Commercial Operating Model (if adopted), the application should ask directly about ability to commit to the actual weekly structure (Saturday morning + weeknight availability) rather than a generic "consistent presence" question — concrete scheduling questions surface real logistics conflicts before an interview is scheduled, saving Rafael's time.

**Confidence built:** That the application itself is being taken seriously — the existing "sit with what you wrote, it will be the first thing you're asked about" confirmation copy is good and should stay.

---

## Stage 6 — The Wait

**What the applicant experiences:** The gap between submitting and hearing back — currently "a few days" per the existing confirmation copy.

**What should resolve here:** Nothing new needs to happen operationally, but this is worth naming as its own stage because it's the one part of the journey with zero design attention anywhere in the repo. An applicant who did everything right and then hears nothing for an ambiguous period is exactly the kind of avoidable uncertainty Hormozi Synthesis #4A exists to eliminate.

**Confidence built:** That silence during this window is expected, not a bad sign — this only works if the pre-application copy (Stage 3, Q1) already set the expectation of "a few days," so the wait itself isn't a new source of doubt.

---

## Stage 7 — The Interview

**What the applicant experiences:** A direct call with Rafael — the real evaluation moment, per the existing operational flow.

**What should resolve here:** Per Client Acquisition Architecture Stage 5, this is where the hypothesis about whether the application stage is pulling its weight gets tested. From the applicant's side: whatever genuine uncertainty remains about fit, cost, or commitment should get a direct, real answer here — this is the one point in the journey where scripted copy should matter least and Rafael's direct judgment should matter most.

**Confidence built:** That he's being genuinely evaluated, not sold to — consistent with the Blueprint's stewardship model and the Constitution's copy principle (Rafael as custodian, not salesman).

---

## Stage 8 — The Decision

**What the applicant experiences:** Accept or reject, communicated personally either way, per the existing operational flow (production URLs, personal framing).

**What should resolve here — on rejection specifically, currently undesigned:** Nothing in any document describes what a thoughtful rejection communication should say beyond "polite decline." Given the stewardship framing, a rejected applicant who was seriously considered deserves more than a form rejection — this is a real gap worth flagging for the eventual Welcome-Packet-adjacent rejection counterpart, not designed here.

**On acceptance:** Hands off directly into the First-Year Member Journey's Phase 1 (Threshold) — the acceptance call itself is already identified there as the first "Moment That Matters."

## Comprehensive Question Bank (input to future `KFL-FAQ.md`)

Every real question identified across this document and prior ones, organized by category, not yet answered in published form — this is the raw material for the FAQ deliverable, not the FAQ itself:

**About the practice:** What happens after I apply? What does the first year look like? What is expected of me? How is progress measured? Why is this different from [alternative]? Is this a martial arts school? What if I have no prior training?

**About commitment:** What's the actual weekly time commitment? Do I need to travel? What happens if I miss a session? What if life circumstances change mid-year?

**About cost:** What does this cost? (Currently undisplayed by design — see Offer/Revenue Architecture; the FAQ will need to decide how to handle this question without displaying a number, or displaying one, depending on where the pricing hypothesis lands.) Is there a payment plan? What does membership actually include?

**About lineage and authority:** What is Jui Pai and why does it matter? What's the actual lineage chain? How is this different from other Wing Chun instruction?

**About fit and rejection:** What happens if I'm not accepted? Can I reapply? What disqualifies someone?

**About the institution:** What is the Special Student Association? What is a Chartered Chapter and does it affect me? What is Legacy Hall?
