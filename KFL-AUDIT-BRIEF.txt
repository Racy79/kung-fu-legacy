SECTION 1: PROJECT CONTEXT
==========================================

WHAT IT IS
----------
Kung Fu Legacy is a selective-entry Wing Chun brotherhood and internal school.
NOT a gym. NOT a coaching program. NOT a class-based martial arts school.
Closest analogues: Freemasonry, a fraternal order, a guild.
Wing Chun is the discipline structure that binds the brotherhood together.
3-year goal: a small group of structured, reliable men who embody the system —
some prepared to carry the lineage forward.

LINEAGE
-------
Yip Man → Moy Yat → Moy Don (Thornton Williams) → Rafael Gonzalez (Moy Don Xùn)
Structure name: Moy Yat Moy Don Ving Tsun Special Student Association

THE MODEL
---------
- 1-on-1 weekly or bi-weekly mentorship + weekly group session
- Maximum 10–20 men at a time (site says maximum fifteen)
- Progression-based: Siu Nim Tau → Chum Kiu → Biu Jee → Muk Yan Jong → Long Pole → Double Knives
- Hong bao (offering) at each progression milestone:
    Siu Nim Tau: $100 / Chum Kiu: $200 / Biu Jee: $300
    Muk Yan Jong: $400 / Long Pole: $500 / Double Knives: $600
    Total progression path: $2,100
- Bai Si ceremony (indoor disciple initiation): $3,000
    Earned through trust, not purchasable. Rafael decides, not the student.
- Monthly membership: $1,000–$1,500 (Texas) / $400–$800 (Puerto Rico)
- Locations: Converse, TX (primary) + San Juan, Puerto Rico

THE IDEAL MEMBER
----------------
NOT broken men. NOT martial arts hobbyists.

Who he is:
- Deep, profound, serious thinker
- Already capable and smart — seeks elevation, not rescue
- Hungry for real brotherhood with men of the same caliber
- Passionate about kung fu, fitness, nutrition, business, entrepreneurship
- Willing to contribute his skills to the collective good
- The kind of man you trust with your back

What he wants:
- Brotherhood, not a program
- Men who pull each other up
- A structured path with real depth: initiation, progression, belonging, shared mission

Disqualifiers:
- Bad energy
- Resistance to structure
- Unwilling to contribute to the collective

COPY PRINCIPLE — NON-NEGOTIABLE
---------------------------------
Rafael is the custodian and gatekeeper of a lineage.
He is NEVER positioned as a lifestyle guru, status superior, or life coach.
His domain authority is Wing Chun and the Moy Yat lineage ONLY.
All copy must stay inside that boundary.

PRICE POINT
-----------
$1,000–$1,500/month.
This is a high-ticket brotherhood offer.
The site must attract and qualify men serious enough to pay that without flinching.
It is NOT selling martial arts classes.

DESIGN SYSTEM
-------------
Dark luxury aesthetic.
Colors: #0B0B0B (matte black), #2B1F1A (espresso), #C6A962 (gold), #E8E3D9 (bone)
Typography: serif for emotional/voice lines, sans for labels/functional text
Gold used for emphasis, italics for voice/tone lines

SECTION 4: AUDIT INSTRUCTIONS
==========================================

Audit this site section by section in page order.

For each section evaluate against:

1. POSITIONING CLARITY
Does this section communicate brotherhood and lineage,
or does it accidentally read like a martial arts gym or coaching offer?

2. IDEAL CLIENT MATCH
Would a serious, capable, high-earning man feel recognized and drawn in,
or would he feel like he is being sold to?

3. COPY QUALITY
Is the language precise and authoritative?
Does it respect the reader's intelligence?
Does it stay within Rafael's domain authority (Wing Chun + lineage only)?

4. CONVERSION LOGIC
Does the page build desire, qualify the reader, and move him toward requesting entry?
Are there sections that slow conversion or create confusion?

5. FRICTION POINTS
Anything that would cause the right man to leave,
or the wrong man to feel welcome.

6. MISSING ELEMENTS
What is absent that the ideal client would need to see before requesting entry?

7. FORM AUDIT
Evaluate the application form specifically:
- Does it feel like a brotherhood entry process or a standard web form?
- Are the questions right for this offer and this man?
- Is anything missing, redundant, or tone-inconsistent?

OUTPUT FORMAT:
Go section by section in page order.
For each section: what works / what to change / specific recommendation.
End with a ranked priority list: top 5 changes that would most impact
conversion for this specific offer and this specific man.

Do not give generic website advice.
Every recommendation must be specific to this offer, this price point, and this ideal client.
