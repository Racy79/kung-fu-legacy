# Kung Fu Legacy — Commercial Operating Model
**Status:** Design memorandum, Version 1 — **not yet adopted policy.** Documents Rafael's proposed shift from an instructional-hours model to a membership-in-apprenticeship model, pressure-tests it against the Institutional Blueprint, Hormozi Synthesis, Offer Architecture, Revenue Architecture, and Client Acquisition Architecture, and lists what would need to change elsewhere if adopted. Nothing in the existing commercial documents has been rewritten yet — this document identifies what *would* need to change, pending confirmation.
**Pre-flight review completed:** all five prior commercial/foundational documents, per the standing workflow.

---

## Part I — The Shift, Restated Precisely

The unit of value moves from **instructional hours purchased** to **membership in a guided apprenticeship**, with instruction as one expression of that membership rather than the product itself. Practically: a Member evaluates "do I belong to something worth supporting," not "did I get enough lesson time."

**Proposed Version 1 operating structure:**
- **Weekly Apprenticeship Session** — Saturday, ~9:00–11:30am, ~2.5 hours. Collective. Principles taught, corrections made, applications explored, discussion happens.
- **Individual Guidance Sessions** — Tuesday and Thursday evenings, 45-minute scheduled appointments. Purpose: review assigned work, correct structural errors, answer questions, assign the next stage of deliberate practice. Explicitly *not* a second complete lesson — closer to mentoring than private instruction.
- **Uniform V1 experience** — every Member receives the same core package (Weekly Apprenticeship Session, regularly scheduled Individual Guidance Sessions, Development Reviews, Legacy Hall, institutional privileges, community, shared standards). No differentiated service tiers until objective advancement criteria exist.

**Proposed terminology:** Weekly Apprenticeship Session, Individual Guidance Session, Development Review, Quarterly Intensive, Legacy Hall, Apprenticeship Cohort — replacing "private lesson," "group class," "lesson package," "hours included." "Individual Guidance Session" specifically preferred over "Founder Session" because it scales honestly to future Chartered Chapters (any Head Instructor can offer one; only Rafael could offer a "Founder Session").

## Part II — Pressure Test Against the Institutional Blueprint

**Strategic Filter (Part I of the Blueprint) — passes cleanly on all three questions.** Preserves instructional quality (uniform experience, no subjective tiering yet). Increases freedom over complexity (the group Weekly Apprenticeship Session serves multiple Members per fixed 2.5-hour block — this is a second, previously undiscussed lever for improving the Freedom Ratio defined in Revenue Architecture Part V, alongside instructor development). Strengthens the body of work future instructors inherit (a repeatable two-session-type structure is more transmissible than ad hoc private lessons that live only in Rafael's calendar).

**Founder-dependency test (Blueprint Part VIII) — this is the strongest convergence in the whole memo.** The Blueprint's own test for founder dependency is whether a Head Instructor "working only from the documents" can produce materially the same experience. Rejecting "Founder Session" for "Individual Guidance Session" is a direct, independently-reasoned application of that exact test to commercial language — Rafael arrived at the Blueprint's own founder-dependency logic without being pointed back to it. Worth stating plainly: this is evidence the Blueprint's principles are actually operating as intended, not just sitting in a frozen document.

**Authority-abuse / institutional-drift failure mode (Blueprint Part VIII) — the "no differentiated tiers until objective standards exist" decision is the correct answer to a failure mode the Blueprint names explicitly.** "This student needs less attention" or "I think this person is ready" as subjective calls is precisely the "quality eroding through growth" and inconsistency risk Part VIII warns about. Deferring differentiation until the Invisible Curriculum, Development Review process, and a Member Development Model produce objective criteria is the right sequencing — not a compromise, the correct order of operations.

**Immutable vs. Adaptable (Blueprint Part III) — the memo applies this test correctly, independently.** "If future experience demonstrates that thirty or sixty minutes produces better results, the pedagogy should determine the duration rather than the commercial model" is exactly the Blueprint's own line ("does changing it change what the Work is, or only how it's delivered"). Formal classification for the record: **specific day/time/duration is adaptable** (logistics, pedagogy-revisable); **the two-session-type structure itself (one collective developmental session + individual guidance producing a specific next assignment) is closer to the curriculum's technical spine** — it encodes a real belief about how transmission works (collective immersion plus individualized refinement) and shouldn't be casually restructured the way a start time can be.

**No contradictions found against the Blueprint.**

## Part III — Pressure Test Against the Hormozi Synthesis

**Value Equation (#4) — the shift directly improves two of the four drivers.** Dream Outcome gains a status/belonging dimension (membership in something, not a la carte hours — Hormozi's own Value Equation entry names status as the usual underlying driver of stated desires). Effort & Sacrifice drops on the psychological axis specifically: "did I get my money's worth in hours" is exactly the kind of transactional counting friction the Value Equation's effort/sacrifice driver penalizes, and membership framing removes it.

**Charge What It's Worth / Virtuous Cycle of Price (#3) — reinforces rather than conflicts.** Commoditized hourly billing was already flagged in Offer Architecture as adjacent to the "starving crowd vs. commodity" distinction Hormozi opens the whole book with (Hormozi Synthesis #1's source chapter: "Pricing — The Commodity Problem"). Membership-in-apprenticeship is the correct structural fix, not just better copy — it changes what's actually being sold, not merely how it's described.

**"Increasing perceived value without necessarily increasing labor" — Rafael's own framing of the Hormozi connection is accurate, and there's a concrete new mechanism for it.** The Weekly Apprenticeship Session is genuinely labor-efficient in a way pure 1:1 private instruction never was: Rafael's per-session labor stays roughly fixed at 2.5 hours regardless of whether 5 or 12 Members attend, while the earlier Revenue Architecture model (Part V, Freedom Model) only had one lever for improving the Freedom Ratio — instructor development. **This is a second lever, available immediately, that Revenue Architecture didn't have when it was written.** This should be added there (see Part V below).

**No contradictions found against the Hormozi Synthesis.**

## Part IV — Contradictions and Reconciliations With Existing Commercial Documents

### Reconciliation needed, not a true contradiction: Revenue Architecture Part III's "per-student-hour" language

Revenue Architecture Part III currently states: "membership economics for KFL should be modeled per-student-hour, not per-month — the real constraint is Rafael's finite correction/evaluation capacity." Read against this memo, that sentence risks being misread as arguing for exactly the hourly-billing framing the memo rejects. **It isn't actually in conflict** — that sentence was about Rafael's *internal* capacity math (how much of his time a given member consumes), not about *member-facing* pricing language. The two are compatible: internally model capacity in hours (for planning), externally frame value in membership terms (for perception). **This needs a clarifying edit in Revenue Architecture**, not a reversal — the internal/external distinction should be stated explicitly there so a future reader doesn't see the same tension this document just resolved.

### Real update needed: Revenue Architecture Part IV's capacity formula was single-tier; the actual model is two-tier

Part IV's formula (hours available ÷ hours per student) assumed an undifferentiated 1:1 model. The proposed structure is genuinely two different capacity constraints:
- **Group capacity** (Weekly Apprenticeship Session): bounded by room/attention span, not by a simple hours-per-student division — a fixed 2.5-hour weekly block can serve a range of Member counts before quality degrades, and that range is a pedagogical judgment (how many people can Rafael meaningfully correct in one session), not a pure math constraint.
- **Individual capacity** (Tuesday/Thursday Individual Guidance Sessions): a hard, calculable constraint — 45-minute slots × available evening hours on two fixed days per week. This is now genuinely computable once the actual window lengths are known (see Part VI, open questions).

**This is a real, useful update to Revenue Architecture Part IV, not just a formality — the individual-session capacity constraint is now more precisely knowable than it was when that document was written**, because "45 minutes, Tuesday and Thursday evenings" is concrete in a way "some hours per month" wasn't.

### No contradiction, but needs sequencing note: Revenue Architecture Part V's premium-tier idea

Part V (adapting Hormozi's Anchor logic) suggested KFL should eventually have "a genuinely premium tier... available to the small number of students who could go deeper." The memo's Long-Term Possibility section (Founder Workshop, Pressure Testing days) is consistent with this, not a rejection of it — it's the same idea, deferred to the same point the memo itself specifies (once objective advancement criteria exist). **No edit needed beyond noting in Revenue Architecture that this is explicitly a post-V1 item**, which the memo already states clearly.

### Opportunity, not a contradiction: Offer Architecture's "first year" gap (Part III, Q2) is now partially closed

Offer Architecture flagged "what does the first year actually look like" as the single highest-priority content gap, unanswerable from any existing material. **This memo answers the weekly-cadence layer of that question** (Saturday collective session + Tuesday/Thursday individual guidance) even though the developmental-arc/milestone layer (what changes month to month) is still open. Offer Architecture Part III, Q2 should be updated to reflect this partial resolution once the operating model is confirmed.

### Opportunity: Client Acquisition Architecture's Onboarding and Trust stages gain concrete content

Client Acquisition Architecture's Trust stage cited "what is expected of me, concretely?" as unanswered. It can now be answered with an actual weekly structure instead of institutional tone alone. Its Onboarding stage flagged the same first-weeks design gap Offer Architecture did — same partial resolution applies.

## Part V — Recommended Update to Revenue Architecture Part V (Freedom Model)

Pending confirmation of this memo, Revenue Architecture Part V should be amended to state a second Freedom Ratio lever explicitly: the Weekly Apprenticeship Session's group format improves the ratio (revenue ÷ personal hours) immediately, without waiting for a second instructor to exist — a nearer-term lever than instructor development, which Part V had previously identified as the *only* structural lever available.

## Part VI — Open Questions Requiring Rafael's Input Before This Is Finalized

1. **Individual Guidance Session frequency, per Member.** Does every Member get one 45-minute slot per week (choosing Tuesday or Thursday), or could/should a Member have both? This directly changes both the capacity formula and, potentially, the pricing hypothesis (more contact time per week than a typical private-lesson comparison would suggest — possible upward evidence for the $1,500–$2,500 hypothesis, not a decision this document should make).
2. **"Apprenticeship Cohort" — does this imply a move to cohort-based intake** (Members joining together on a cycle, e.g., quarterly) as opposed to the current rolling individual-application model? If so, this materially changes Client Acquisition Architecture's Application/Acceptance stages and would make Hormozi Synthesis #8's "cohort-based rolling urgency" (previously reserved only for gatherings/chapter cycles, never admission) newly relevant and worth revisiting — but only if cohort intake is actually intended, not assumed here.
3. **"Quarterly Intensive"** appears in the preferred-terminology list but not in the V1 Membership Experience deliverables list. Is it a V1 offering or a later-phase item, like the Long-Term Possibility Saturdays illustration?
4. **Actual Tuesday/Thursday evening window lengths** — needed to complete the individual-session capacity calculation in Part IV above (e.g., a 3-hour evening window yields 4 slots per day at 45 minutes each; the real number is unknown here).
