# Kung Fu Legacy — First-Year Member Journey
**Status:** Working document, Execution Mode (Phase II). A transformation map, not a syllabus or a calendar — describes what changes in a Member over the first year, not a list of activities. Directly addresses Offer Architecture's flagged gap (Part III, Q2: "what does the first year actually look like").
**Pre-flight review completed:** Institutional Blueprint (custodial-character observation windows, Part VIII), Hormozi Synthesis (#6 Fast Wins, #4A Reducing Uncertainty), Offer Architecture (Value Equation applied to KFL), Revenue Architecture, Client Acquisition Architecture, Commercial Operating Model (not yet adopted — this document assumes its Weekly Apprenticeship Session / Individual Guidance Session structure as the working backdrop, since it's the best available description of the actual experience; if the Operating Model changes, this document's phase content should be re-checked, not just its schedule references).
**A sequencing note, acknowledged rather than silently followed:** the Institutional Blueprint's own sequencing logic (Part III) argued the Invisible Curriculum should exist before content like this is built, to avoid encoding untested pedagogical assumptions. Rafael's explicit instruction here — build the experience first, let the offer derive from it — reverses that order deliberately. That's a reasonable methodological choice, not an oversight, and is noted here so it isn't mistaken for one later. This document should be checked against the Invisible Curriculum once it exists, not treated as having pre-empted it.
**A second honest caveat:** no real student data exists anywhere in this repository (Assumption Register A5, A16). Everything below is designed content — a reasoned map, not a validated one. It should be treated as a hypothesis the first validated-learning cohort (Client Acquisition Architecture) will actually test, not as an established curriculum.

## Framework

Five phases, boundaries drawn from developmental logic already established elsewhere in this project — specifically the custodial-character observation windows already defined in Blueprint Part VIII ("Early visible: 3 months... Mid-term: 1–3 years") — rather than invented fresh. Each phase answers five questions: what the Member experiences, what uncertainty disappears, what confidence is gained, what responsibility increases, and what institutional attachment develops.

---

## Phase 1 — Threshold (Weeks 1–2)

**What the Member experiences:** The transition from applicant to Member. The personal acceptance call. The onboarding sequence (Blueprint Part VII — Constitution first, then Covenant, then practical forms). The first Weekly Apprenticeship Session — walking into a room of people already further along, for the first time.

**What uncertainty disappears:** Everything Offer Architecture Part III identified as unanswered before this point — what happens after acceptance, what's expected, what the weekly rhythm actually is — should already be resolved by now if the pre-application copy did its job. What disappears *here* specifically is the abstractness of it: the Member now has a concrete Saturday morning and two concrete weeknight windows instead of a described structure.

**What confidence is gained:** Almost none yet, and that's correct — this phase is about orientation, not competence. The one thing a Member should feel confident about by the end of week two is that he was right to apply — that the room matches what he was told it would be.

**What responsibility increases:** From zero to "show up prepared and present." Nothing more is expected yet.

**What institutional attachment develops:** The first sense of having crossed a real threshold — not a purchase confirmation, an admission. This is the phase where the Constitution-first onboarding sequence (Blueprint Part VII) does its heaviest lifting: reading the governing document before the practical forms frames everything that follows as entry into something, not signup for something.

---

## Phase 2 — Foundation (Months 1–3)

**What the Member experiences:** Repetition of structural basics inside the Weekly Apprenticeship Session, paired with Individual Guidance Sessions that are, correctly, narrow in scope — each one should produce exactly one clear next thing to work on, per the Commercial Operating Model's explicit design intent, not a second full lesson's worth of material.

**What uncertainty disappears:** Whether he can actually be corrected without ego resistance — this is the first real test of the qualifier he answered honestly (or didn't) on the application. Also: what "development being tracked" actually feels like, once the first Development Review happens.

**What confidence is gained:** The first genuine felt competence — not mastery, a specific, nameable improvement he can point to. This is where the Fast Win principle (Hormozi Synthesis #6) has to land or the whole phase fails: without an early, real, feel-able correction landing successfully, everything downstream is working against a foundation of doubt instead of evidence.

**What responsibility increases:** From "show up prepared" to "do the assigned work between sessions." The Individual Guidance Session's core mechanic — leave knowing exactly what to work on next — only works if the Member actually does that work; this phase is where that discipline either forms or doesn't.

**What institutional attachment develops:** Recognition of the other Members in the room as fixed points, not strangers — the beginning of "this room" as a specific place rather than "a place like this."

**Moment That Matters — the First Correction That Lands.** Not the first correction chronologically; the first one that produces a visible, felt "oh" — the specific moment the abstract promise of individualized attention becomes concrete. This should be treated as a real, trackable milestone, not left to chance.

---

## Phase 3 — Friction (Months 3–6)

**What the Member experiences:** The plateau. Early-win momentum fades; the gap between where he is and where the Dream Outcome sits becomes more visible than the early progress was. This is also, not coincidentally, the exact window the Blueprint's custodial-character framework names as the first real observation point — "response to plateau" is explicitly listed there as a mid-term indicator, which means this phase is doing real institutional work even though the Member experiences it as simply hard.

**What uncertainty disappears:** Whether commitment was real or situational — his own, not just what he told Rafael in the application. This is the phase a Member who applied for the wrong reasons (status-seeking, novelty) typically reveals it, to himself as much as to anyone watching.

**What confidence is gained:** Almost none from technique — that's the point of a plateau. What should be gained instead is a different kind of confidence: that the standard doesn't move to accommodate frustration, and that this is trustworthy rather than punishing. This is a hard needle to thread and depends entirely on how Individual Guidance Sessions are actually run during this window — the same 45-minute structure that felt generative in Phase 2 needs to feel steady, not repetitive, here.

**What responsibility increases:** From "do the assigned work" to "keep showing up without being told to." The Weekly Apprenticeship Session's group format matters specifically here — a Member who might quietly disengage from a purely 1:1 relationship has to actively not-show-up from a room other people are used to seeing him in, which is a real (if soft) accountability structure the pure private-lesson model didn't have.

**What institutional attachment develops:** The first real test of whether "membership in something worth supporting" (the Commercial Operating Model's central premise) actually holds under difficulty, or whether it collapses back into "am I getting my money's worth in hours" the moment things get hard. If the membership framing is working, this is the phase that proves it.

**Moment That Matters — Being Seen Struggling, Without It Costing Him Standing.** However this actually happens (a word from Rafael, a peer's small gesture, simply not being treated differently on a bad week), the plateau phase needs at least one concrete moment that proves struggle doesn't reduce belonging. This is currently undesigned — flagged here as something the eventual Invisible Curriculum or Instructor Manual should address deliberately, not leave to instinct alone.

---

## Phase 4 — Integration (Months 6–9)

**What the Member experiences:** Technique and principle starting to connect rather than sitting as separate lessons — the thing Hormozi's Value Equation calls "Perceived Likelihood of Achievement" shifting from borrowed (trusting Rafael's lineage credibility) to earned (his own accumulating evidence).

**What uncertainty disappears:** Whether this actually works for *him* specifically, as opposed to working in the abstract or for other people in the room.

**What confidence is gained:** Real, first-person evidence — not "I was told this works," but "I've now watched it work in me."

**What responsibility increases:** The first informal, low-stakes moments of helping someone newer — not a formal role, just what naturally happens when someone six months in shares a room with someone three weeks in. This matters institutionally: "behavior toward beginners" is explicitly named in the Blueprint as one of the early-visible custodial-character indicators, and this is the first phase where it's actually observable, not just theoretical.

**What institutional attachment develops:** From "I attend this" to "I'm part of this" — the shift from consumer identity to member identity the whole Commercial Operating Model is designed to produce. If it hasn't happened by month nine, that's a meaningful signal, not just a slow starter.

**Moment That Matters — First Time Correcting or Encouraging a Newer Member.** Unprompted, not assigned. The first visible sign the Member has something to contribute, not just receive — the beginning of the disposition the succession pipeline eventually depends on, though this should never be communicated to the Member as evaluation (per Blueprint Part VIII's non-disclosure principle) — it should simply be allowed to happen naturally and be quietly noticed.

---

## Phase 5 — Ownership (Months 9–12)

**What the Member experiences:** The apprenticeship starting to feel like an identity rather than an activity. First real exposure to institutional privileges beyond the weekly structure — Legacy Hall depth, historical/archive material, possibly a Founder dinner or the first annual gathering if the calendar lines up within the year.

**What uncertainty disappears:** Whether he'll still be doing this in year two — by month twelve this should no longer be a live question for a Member who's actually integrated; if it still is, that's diagnostic, not neutral.

**What confidence is gained:** Ownership of his own development — less dependent on being told what to work on, more able to identify it himself, even though Individual Guidance Sessions continue to assign the formal next step.

**What responsibility increases:** From informal helping (Phase 4) to something closer to a standing expectation that he holds the room's standard alongside Rafael, not just receives it — without any formal title or announced status, consistent with the Blueprint's "never communicated" succession principle.

**What institutional attachment develops:** The first real sense of being inside the lineage's continuity, not just under its instruction — the difference between "I am learning Wing Chun" and "I am part of how this gets carried forward." This is the correct, quiet endpoint of a first year — not a credential, a felt shift in identity.

**Moment That Matters — The First Development Review That Talks About Direction, Not Just Progress.** Earlier Development Reviews (Phase 2 onward) should be about technical/developmental tracking. The twelve-month one is the natural point for the conversation to widen — not a promise of anything, not a hint at instructor-track status, simply the first time the conversation treats him as someone with a future in this, not just a present in it.

## Moments That Matter — Full List

Consolidated from the phases above, for use in designing the actual Member experience and, later, the Instructor Manual:

1. The Acceptance Call (Threshold)
2. First Weekly Apprenticeship Session (Threshold)
3. The First Correction That Lands (Foundation)
4. First Development Review (Foundation)
5. Being Seen Struggling Without It Costing Standing (Friction) — currently undesigned, flagged for future work
6. First Unprompted Act of Helping a Newer Member (Integration)
7. First Exposure to Deeper Institutional Privileges — Legacy Hall, archive, Founder dinner, gathering (Ownership)
8. The Twelve-Month Development Review That Widens to Direction (Ownership)

## What This Document Does Not Do

- It does not promise or hint at succession-track status at any point — every reference to increasing responsibility or institutional attachment is deliberately framed as belonging, not advancement toward a title, consistent with Blueprint Part VIII's non-disclosure principle.
- It does not replace the Invisible Curriculum — it's a provisional experience map that the Invisible Curriculum should eventually formalize or correct, not the other way around.
- It does not commit to any of the Commercial Operating Model's specifics (session length, cadence) as final — those remain open per that document's Part VI.
