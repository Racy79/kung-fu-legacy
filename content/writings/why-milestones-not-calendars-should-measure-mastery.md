---
title: "Why Milestones, Not Calendars, Should Measure Mastery"
date: "2026-08-28"
register: "tradition"
excerpt: "Time in the room is not the same thing as time actually spent developing."
---

Most systems measure progress by time. Six months to this level, two years to that one, a schedule that exists whether or not the person moving through it actually developed anything along the way. I understand the appeal. A calendar is predictable, easy to sell, easy to plan around. It's also, in my experience, one of the more dishonest ways to measure whether someone actually became what the next rank claims they became.

Two people can spend the exact same eighteen months training. One of them trains present, correcting real habits, absorbing what's actually being taught instead of just performing the motions well enough to pass. The other trains on autopilot, technically attending, technically repeating the forms, and quietly plateauing somewhere around month four without anyone noticing because attendance kept looking the same on paper. A calendar-based system promotes both of these people at the same time, because time is the only thing it's actually measuring.

Milestones don't care how long something took. They care whether the thing actually happened. Can this person maintain structure under real pressure, not just in a drill where everyone's cooperating. Can they recover when their plan already failed. Do they understand why a technique works, not just how to perform it when asked. Those are yes-or-no questions, not calendar questions, and they don't care whether the answer took four months or four years to become true.

This makes milestone-based development slower to promise and harder to sell, because you can't hand someone a date and let them count down to it. You can only tell them what has to actually be true about their skill before they move forward, and then wait, honestly, until it's true. Some people get there quickly. Some take much longer, for reasons that have nothing to do with effort and everything to do with how their body and mind happen to absorb this specific kind of training. Neither of those students is doing anything wrong. They're just on their own actual timeline instead of a manufactured one.

I'd rather tell a student the truth about where they are than hand them a rank that flatters a calendar. A rank that isn't real yet doesn't help anyone, least of all the person wearing it.

-R Gonzalez
