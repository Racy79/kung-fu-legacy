## Part I — Business Model

The Blueprint (Part IX) already establishes the objective correctly: not growth, but long-term financial independence across multiple complementary income sources, evaluated by freedom-per-hour-invested. This document's job is to turn that philosophy into an actual sequenced model with real mechanics — the thing the Blueprint deliberately deferred.

**The seven income sources, now sequenced** (applying Hormozi Synthesis #12's sequencing discipline to Blueprint Part IX's table):

| Stage | Source | Why it comes at this stage |
|---|---|---|
| **Now — foundation** | Private apprenticeship | Already exists, already the highest-fidelity source, funds everything else, and is the only source that directly produces the Invisible Curriculum's raw material (real teaching, real correction, real problems encountered) |
| **Next — unlocked by the Invisible Curriculum** | Student Manual, Curriculum Map | Byproducts of writing the Invisible Curriculum for internal use — near-zero marginal cost to formalize once it exists |
| **Then — requires the Invisible Curriculum to exist first** | Books | Directly monetizes the documented philosophy; can't be written honestly before the underlying curriculum is itself written |
| **Then — requires proof, not just content** | Online courses | Needs either real student outcomes or at minimum a complete curriculum to avoid selling an unproven system at scale |
| **Parallel, not sequential — starts as soon as a second qualified person exists** | Instructor development | Doesn't wait for the other stages; begins the moment any current student is ready, per the Blueprint's succession model |
| **Later — needs a real community to gather** | Annual gatherings | Requires enough current + former students that a gathering is a real event, not a founder dinner with two people |
| **Last — the long-term structure, not the near-term plan** | Chartered Chapter contributions | Explicitly requires a Head Instructor to exist first, which requires instructor development to have already produced one — this is a five-to-ten-year horizon item, not a near-term revenue line |

**What this sequencing actually argues:** the single highest-leverage near-term action for revenue diversification is not a new income source at all — it's writing the Invisible Curriculum, because three of the seven sources (Student Manual, Curriculum Map, Books) are directly blocked on it and a fourth (Courses) is blocked on it indirectly. This confirms rather than changes the Blueprint's own Part X sequencing conclusion — Revenue Architecture doesn't discover a new priority, it explains in commercial terms why the Blueprint's existing priority is also the correct commercial priority.

## Part II — Pricing Philosophy

Per Hormozi Synthesis #3 (Virtuous Cycle of Price) and Offer Architecture Part I (the category-of-one differentiation argument): KFL should not price by looking at competitor martial-arts-school rates and adjusting. It should price at whatever level produces maximum genuine investment from the right person, because investment level is causally linked to outcome quality — underpricing isn't generous, it's counterproductive.

**What a defensible price has to account for, structurally:**
1. Rafael's actual available correction/evaluation time per student per month (currently undocumented — see Assumption Register A2)
2. The opportunity cost of that time against his other income (corporate job, real estate — currently undocumented)
3. What "sting" looks like for the actual target buyer (entrepreneur/executive, $X+ income — profile documented in `KFL-PROFESSIONAL-BRIEF.md`, but no updated income data since the April brotherhood-era targeting)
4. The existing anchor: $1,000/mo is live in Stripe today

**A hypothesis, not a recommendation.** Given the target buyer profile (established, high-earning professionals for whom $1,000–$1,500/mo was previously assessed as "not a shock if value and exclusivity are clear") and Hormozi's argument that underpricing actively degrades outcomes, one directional read is that **the live $1,000/mo price is more likely too low than too high** for a private, individually-corrected apprenticeship with a genuine multi-generational lineage credential behind it. But pricing logic from a single principle (Charge What It's Worth) is not sufficient grounds for a number — the correct price is downstream of the completed Offer Architecture, the Member Experience documents (not yet written), and this Revenue Architecture together, not any one of them in isolation. $1,500–$2,500/mo is therefore recorded here as a **testable hypothesis** to validate through the acquisition process (see Client Acquisition Architecture's validated-learning objective), not a figure Rafael should treat as settled. The commercial architecture as a whole should justify the eventual price; this document alone should not.

**What this document explicitly does not decide:** the hong bao/Bai Si progression-payment structure. Per Rafael's own instruction, that stays deferred and un-assumed until reconsidered separately from first principles.

## Part III — Membership Economics

**Cost side.** The primary cost of private apprenticeship is Rafael's time, not materials or facility (per V1-BASELINE's "the garage is not the offer" design decision — there's no premium-facility cost structure to account for). This means margin is almost entirely a function of price × hours-per-student, not a function of reducing delivery cost — which is structurally the opposite of a typical service business and argues strongly against ever discounting (there's no "spread fixed costs over more volume" logic available here; more students at a lower price is strictly worse than fewer students at a higher price, for both margin and — per the Virtuous Cycle of Price — outcome quality).

**Value side.** Per Offer Architecture Part II, the Perceived Likelihood of Achievement driver is currently weak (no visible evidence of student outcomes). This is a commercial problem, not just a copy problem: weak likelihood perception caps the price a rational prospect will accept regardless of how the price itself is framed. **Fixing the evidence gap (real testimonials, a described track record, general Plum Blossom framework visibility) is a precondition for successfully raising price, not a parallel workstream.**

**Structural implication:** membership economics for KFL should be modeled per-student-hour, not per-month — the real constraint is Rafael's finite correction/evaluation capacity, and any pricing model that doesn't explicitly account for that constraint will eventually either overcommit his time or underprice his differentiated value.

## Part IV — Capacity Model (Honest Scarcity)

Per Hormozi Synthesis #7: capacity should be a real, transparently stated number derived from actual evaluation bandwidth — not a marketing-chosen figure like the historical "fifteen."

**The honest calculation this requires:** capacity = (Rafael's weekly hours available for private instruction + evaluation) ÷ (hours required per student to deliver genuine correction and assess custodial-character development). Neither number is documented anywhere in this repository. The historical "fifteen" figure was never shown to derive from this calculation — it reads, on the evidence in the Current State Assessment, as a round marketing number from the April brotherhood-era positioning, which is itself part of why it was quietly dropped from the live site.

**What this document can responsibly say:** a real capacity number requires Rafael to supply (a) how many hours per week he can sustainably commit to private instruction alongside his other income sources, and (b) his own estimate of hours-per-student-per-month needed for real correction (not just a session length — actual attention including between-session availability). Until those two inputs exist, any capacity figure in future site copy is provisional, and "Honest Scarcity" cannot honestly be practiced yet — you can't transparently state a real constraint you haven't calculated.

**Recommendation:** don't restate a capacity number publicly until this calculation is done. Silence on capacity is more honest than restating "fifteen" without knowing whether it's still true.

## Part V — Freedom Model

Per the Blueprint's Strategic Filter (question 2) and Part IX: the test for any revenue decision is whether it increases freedom-per-hour-invested, not whether it increases revenue in isolation.

**The formula this implies**, stated explicitly for the first time:

```
Freedom Contribution of a Source = (Revenue from Source) ÷ (Rafael's ongoing personal hours required to sustain it)
```

Private apprenticeship scores lowest on this ratio long-term (revenue scales linearly with Rafael's personal hours — the definition of the dependency the whole Blueprint exists to escape) even though it's the highest-*fidelity* source today. Books and courses score highest (revenue continues with near-zero ongoing personal hours once built). Instructor development is the pivot point: it costs personal hours now but is the only source that eventually *reduces* the denominator on private-apprenticeship-equivalent revenue (a trained instructor teaching under the same standard produces Work-preserving revenue without consuming Rafael's own hours).

**What this argues, concretely:** the freedom objective isn't served by adding more private students at the current model — it's served by (a) pricing existing private apprenticeship at a level that reflects its true differentiated value (Part II) rather than expanding its volume, and (b) prioritizing instructor development earlier than its position in the Blueprint's income-source table might suggest, specifically because it's the only lever that changes the freedom ratio structurally rather than just adding a new numerator.
